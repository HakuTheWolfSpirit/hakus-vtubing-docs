# Basics for 3D VTubing

When it comes to 3d VTubing there are multiple ways you can approach this.

- Comission a custom 3D VTubing model
- Create your own model in blender
- Use VRoid to create an avatar
- Buy what you need on a market place

Each of these approaches has advantages and disadvantages and what you should choose depends on how much time, money and skill you have or are able to acquire. 

## Comissioning a 3D VTubing model

Comissioning a 3D Model is the most expensive option out of all of these. You have to do nothing but imagine what you want. If you have no idea what you want I recommend you not going this path. The result you can get with this are pretty good for the effort you need to do, but its not very flexible. You will be stuck with whatever result you get out of this for a long time. And if there is something you'd like to have changed you most likely need to comission the change too. You buy a finished product.

## Creating your own model in blender

This one costs you absolutely nothing. Except for your time. In theory there is no upper limit of what you can do. Everything is depending on how good you can handle the program, the technique and the art. You need need to learn how to model, rig, texture. You need to learn how to make the model in a way other programs can understand it. And if you are honest with yourself: the result will likely look mediocre or even bad. This option should be picked by creative people with thousands of hours available for their projects. Modeling 10 hours on weekends will likely not yield good results and probably frustrate you and make you give up. Please do not overestimate the complexity of this as you will likely learn all the incompatibilities after you have already invested a lot of time into this. This is a path you can go after you already understand how everything works and can be built from scratch. 

## VRoid

This option is the easiest one and the cheapest one to get a simple model out. You drag some sliders to modify your body shape, put on some pre made clothes, modify some textures and export your file as .VRM file. The .VRM file is a standardized file format specifically for models made in Roid. It is free and straight forward to use. But you will be limited to what this toolset offers you. If you can paint textures you will get pretty far with this and you have the option to purchase some clothing too. The program differentiates between different types of clothes (like dress, underweaer or shoes) so you can combine things how you want to. The .VRM format is supported by all major softwares (except for VR Chat, but that does not stop you from importing it there too). 

## Marketplace avatar

With this you buy your components from a market place. 

Two major marketplaces are currently available for purchasing components

- Jinxxy (little bit Furry targeted)
- BOOTH (japanese only)

With this approach you essentially purchase an avatar base, look up clothes that fit your chosen avatar base and slap them on. Exporting to VR Chat is trivial as this approach is VR Chat native, but exporting for other programs requires some extra effort. 

This is the approach I recommend for the best results for beginners.
